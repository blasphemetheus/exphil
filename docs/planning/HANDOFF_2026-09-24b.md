The margins say the later training *was* buying something the win rate could not

**Confound on the selection numbers: `by_port` is `{'1': 1.0}` — all 60 games
were port 1.** R3 trains port 1 only, so the selection measured the trained side
exclusively. The 200-game test splits ports; **port 2 is the real generalization
question**, and a large p1/p2 gap would mean part of what looks like an exploit
is simply port-specialization. Report the split, never the aggregate alone.

Selection winner: **`head_iter150`** (ties break toward the earlier, less-drifted
head by construction).

**A 100 % win rate means the instrument has run out of resolution.** It can no
longer separate iteration 150 from iteration 300, and it cannot separate "better
at Melee" from "found something this one opponent cannot answer at all." Fox's
91 % still had headroom and was therefore informative; this has none.

Read together with the training curve, this is coherent and unflattering: the
policy narrowed (entropy 2.39 → 1.45) at flat KL, and then beat one fixed
opponent every single time. That is the shape of an exploit. Note also that
**iteration 70 was already at 95 %** — the 230 iterations after it raised
reward/env from ~0.30 to 0.825 while buying almost no additional wins, which is
what you would expect if the later training was sharpening an exploit rather
than improving play.

**Consequences, and they are not optional:**

1. **An opponent pool is now a requirement, not an improvement.** No further
   Mewtwo (or Fox) RL claim from a frozen-ditto win rate means anything.
   `opponent_pool.ex` exists; RL_ON_PRIOR's design section already called for
   "frozen prior, then a pool". This is the evidence that the pool is due.
2. **The remaining useful signals from this arm are SD rate and live play**,
   not win rate. The reported Mewtwo problem was self-destructs; measure that.
3. **Prefer the earlier head.** The selection ties are broken toward the earlier
   checkpoint by construction, and iteration 70 already carried nearly all the
   measurable gain at a third of the drift.
4. This equally undermines reading Fox's 91 % as "the policy is good" — one

## 6. Gotchas learned today

- **`ExPhil.Bridge.SimBatch` is a GenServer wrapper; the implementation and its
  lookup tables live in `ExPhil.Bridge.SimBatch.Core`** (`sim_batch.ex` defines
  `.Core`, `sim_batch_server.ex` defines `SimBatch`). A `def` added to the
  wrong one compiles fine and is undefined at the call site.
- **A wrong assertion looks exactly like a wrong system.** My first admission
  probe failed Mewtwo because I assumed `state.player.character` was an atom;
  it is an integer. The sim had been correct all along. Check the
  representation before believing a red check.
- `:erlang.term_to_binary` on GPU-resident tensors produces a file that cannot
  be decoded back — always `ExPhil.Training.PPO.to_binary_backend/1` first.
- `scripts/launch_unit.sh UNIT '<cmd>'` for anything long; `systemd-run --user`
  starts in `$HOME`, so a bare command dies instantly with no log.
- One EXLA process on the GPU at a time; check `systemctl --user list-units
  'exphil-*'` and the coordination log before starting anything.
