The heads were independent. Conditioning them brings back up‑B.

The 08-29 board ended on "no selector keeps the tail." The answer turned out to be a training change, not a selector: fox_gen_v1's six controller heads were sampled independently, so an up-B — "B and stick-up on the same frame" — only ever happened at the product of its marginals, and the bot drifted to one-head routes (side-B, airdodge) and died on them. A true autoregressive head (buttons → sticks → shoulder, slippi-ai style) was built, tested, and fit on the frozen ep10 trunk in one day. The pre-registered 8a rule fired: SIGNAL. Up-B recovery routes appeared from literally zero, airdodge spam halved, recovery deaths fell 51%→35%. Bradley's live read: structure yes, polish no — "a good player sandbagging." Next: unfreeze the trunk for 3 epochs (plan items 8/9), on a corpus that fixes the other find of the day — 43% of v1's demonstrations weren't Fox. Updated 08-30 late.

Mechanism

Independent heads mathematically suppress coincidence moves

The joint per-frame action was modelled as six independent draws; the error is the total correlation, measured at 0.86 bits/frame in expert play. If P(B)=0.3 and P(up)=0.3 but the expert only presses them together, the independent model gives the true joint 0.09 and invents 0.21 of "B with the stick elsewhere" that never happens. That is A2/C4's airdodge-instead-of-up-B, derived rather than observed.

Method

Two burned 8a runs bought two laws

Run 1 finished a 2.5 h capture then died at save: term_to_binary on a 19 GB map exceeds the BEAM's 2 GB external-term limit → captures are now streamed (raw binaries per replay, gathered by :binary.part). Run 2's bracket: both arms died at ~35 s/game because the heads were fit with plain CE — a refit head must carry the source run's full loss recipe (pos-weights, smoothing, focal, edge, entropy, lr — now read automatically from *_config.json). Val loss looked fine both times; only the live rung caught it.

Latency

AR sampling fused to one XLA program: 2.53 ms/decision

The first sequential sampler (two kernels + eager glue) ran 24 ms/decision — 50% stale sends at 60 Hz, a silent handicap on any live comparison. Hysteresis and argmax-buttons apply before conditioning, so later heads condition on what will actually be pressed.

Where this leaves the program. The 08-29 board's closing problem — "cashing the headroom needs a rule that keeps the tail, and no candidate exists" — got a partial answer from an unexpected direction: some of what looked like a selection failure was the action factorization itself. The AR head is that rare fix that moves picks without concentrating the decode. It is not the whole answer (the C3 dithering and C2 finisher gaps are trunk properties the head can't reach), which is exactly what the unfreeze tests: if 3 epochs of joint training close polish while keeping the recovered structure, v1.1-AR becomes the new base for every parked thread — critic, Leg S, drills.

Watch item for the unfreeze: hold the pre-registered comparison to AR-vs-IND on the same corpus. The v1→v1.1 improvement will confound head and corpus — that was a deliberate choice, recorded here, not an oversight to discover later.

Laws

Standing rules that earned their place by costing something. The older ones (never crown on stand-dummy numbers, pin stages in deciders, chains from ShineChain over replays only, L1–L10 from the 08-29 board) still hold.

L11Never term_to_binary a big capture. The BEAM's external-term limit is 2 GB; a 19 GB map died after the 2.5 h capture. Stream raw binaries per item; gather with :binary.part.
L12A refit head carries the source run's full loss recipe. Plain CE produced plausible val losses and 35-second games. Pos-weights, smoothing, focal, edge, entropy, lr — read from the checkpoint's *_config.json, now automatic.
L13AR sampling stays one fused XLA program. Eager glue between kernels was 24 ms/decision = 50% stale sends. Benchmark any sampler change against the 2.53 ms reference.
L14loop_report means are not robust to one degenerate game. A single 100 KB stub at "3600 taunts/min" turned 2/min into 331/min. Session dirs: stub filter + medians; the stub stays on disk as evidence (L2's cousin — never delete the evidence).
L15Verify a never-exercised code path before betting a run on it. --resume + --head autoregressive would have silently trained the wrong head: the checkpoint's config clobbered the flag. Guard #6's class, caught in precheck this time.
