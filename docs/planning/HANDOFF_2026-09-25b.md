Bradley played both exported heads. `iter300` = roll to the ledge, grab, back
throw; *"only really works against a static opponent like what it had to face"*.
`iter150` *"definitely better"*.

**The evidence was already in the eval output, uncompared.** Means over the 8
fingerprint games per selection arm vs the untouched prior, same evaluator:
`roll_backward` ×13.1, `roll_forward` ×4.9, `grab` ×3.7, `throw_back_mix` ×4.0,
`ledge_roll` 0 → 0.25, **`aerial_per_min` −92 %**. Full table and the detector
rule: `DEGENERATE_ZOO.md` Entry 1.

the prior's 0.39). The human "spot dodging" impression was wrong; it is rolling.
`spotdodge_per_min` stays out of the signature.

### 4.2 The opponent pool worked — and this is the most important result of the day

Astra acted on my ASK 13 and ran a Mewtwo pool arm (`eval_runs/0925_mewtwo_pool`,
unit `exphil-mewtwo-pool-v2`, **complete 18:58**). Pool = frozen prior + v1 heads
70/150/300, with **v1 head100 held out as an unseen opponent**. KL 0.01, ≤200
iterations. Results:

| matchup | record | win rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pool candidate vs **prior** | 99W 1L | 99.0 % — saturated, as before |
| **prior** vs held-out head100 (control) | 5W 95L | **5.0 %** |
| pool candidate vs **held-out head100** | 66W 34L | **66.0 %** |

**Read this carefully, because it is the first Mewtwo number with any resolution
left in it.** The held-out head100 *crushes* the prior 95-5, so it is a far
stronger opponent than the one every previous measurement used. Against it the
pool candidate scores 66 %, not ~100 % — an unsaturated, informative number,
and a very large improvement over the prior's 5 %.

So: **the frozen-ditto gate was saturated (§4.1 of `HANDOFF_2026-09-25.md`), and
a held-out opponent de-saturates it.** That is ASK 13 answered by demonstration
rather than argument.

**What it does NOT establish, and nobody has checked:** whether the pool
candidate is itself degenerate. `find eval_runs/0925_mewtwo_pool -name
degeneracy_report.json` returns **nothing** — the pool arm has not been run
through the checker, and it has not been playtested. A 66 % against a strong
held-out opponent is much harder to fake than 99 % against a weak frozen one,
but "harder to fake" is not "checked". **This is §5 item 1.**
